# ARCSTUDIO91 Content Quality Skill

## Mission
Create original, useful architecture education for @arcstudio91 using free/open-source tools only. Final visual assets MUST be created with locally run open-weight AI models. Do not use image_gen, paid subscriptions, image-generation credits, paid APIs, hosted paid inference, or anything requiring a payment card. Never claim a carousel was published unless the platform confirms it.

## Free production stack
- **Python + Pillow**: compose and export each slide independently at 1080×1350 JPG and PNG master.
- **FLUX.1-schnell** (`black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell`, Apache-2.0 model licence): default still-image model, run locally through Diffusers. Save prompt, seed, model ID, licence, dimensions and output hashes. Verify the upstream model card before changing versions.
- Additional open-weight models may be used only after licence, hardware, provenance and quality are checked. Do not rely on trial API credits or community Spaces for final daily assets.
- **Inkscape**: free vector editing for clean diagrams and simple architectural linework.
- **Blender**: optional free/open-source 3D tool for non-AI geometry studies and layout augmentation; it must not replace the required AI-generated hero imagery in the final pack.
- **GIMP**: free image editing and final visual checks.
- **GitHub Actions**: free-tier concept scheduling only. Final AI inference happens on the user’s local GPU so model use does not depend on paid cloud compute; do not assume unlimited Actions quota.
- **FFmpeg**: optional free tool for future motion/video exports; not needed for a static carousel.

## Research and originality
1. Use the supplied Instagram profile only if public access works. If it cannot be accessed, say so and rely on the stated brand principles without inventing profile observations.
2. Research broad worldwide architecture context using freely accessible sources (e.g., architecture publications, public university resources, standards/guidance from official bodies). Record source URLs and publication dates. Research informs context; do not copy the source's drawings, plans, renders, project compositions, captions, or distinctive details.
3. Create a genuinely original concept and original Marathi + English wording. Rotate typologies globally: homes, apartments, villas, offices, commercial, resorts, hotels, cafés/restaurants, retail, education, healthcare, public space, landscape, and adaptive reuse.
4. Check the recent concept log before drafting. Avoid repeating recent concepts, headlines, images, material lists, and sentence structures. If no reliable history is available, state that repetition checking is limited.
5. Verify factual claims. Separate broadly accepted principles from project-specific assumptions. Do not present design guidance as construction instructions; recommend site-specific review by a qualified local architect/engineer when relevant.

## Free-tool fallback policy

Use Pillow-built typography/layout, original vector diagrams from Inkscape/SVG, or a simple Blender concept model when feasible. If a high-quality result cannot be made from available free assets, stop safely and report the limitation instead of using an unlicensed image or lowering quality silently.

## Premium editorial direction
Treat the account like a thoughtful architecture journal, not a property-advertising feed. Each daily carousel should teach one memorable thing, show a distinct architectural idea, and make a reader want to save or share it. Keep the visuals restrained and warm: natural materials, clear geometry, daylight, honest textures, generous negative space, strong hierarchy, no luxury clichés.

### Rotate content pillars
Rotate broadly across these pillars rather than posting the same building type every day:
1. **Original design studies:** homes, compact apartments, villas, offices, hospitality, retail and mixed-use.
2. **Climate-wise living:** shade, daylight, cross-ventilation, monsoon drainage, heat, orientation and local landscape.
3. **Space that works:** circulation, storage, privacy, accessibility, flexible rooms, service routes and small-space planning.
4. **Durability and care:** repairability, water protection, maintainable facades, replaceable parts, cleaning, safe inspection access and sensible material choices.
5. **People-first places:** clinics, learning spaces, libraries, public seating, community pavilions and inclusive access.
6. **Reuse and regeneration:** adaptive reuse, existing-building audits, retained materials, retrofit sequencing and landscape restoration.
7. **Interiors and sensory design:** acoustics, glare, visual calm, tactile materials, wayfinding and furniture layout.
8. **Landscape and water:** native planting where appropriate, shade trees, permeable surfaces, rain gardens and responsible water management.
9. **Architecture explained:** one myth vs fact, one drawing explained, one site-planning question, one material comparison, or a “what to check before you build” checklist.
10. **Behind the decisions:** concept rationale, trade-offs, cost-aware priorities and the questions an architect asks before choosing a detail. Never invent a client, completed project, budget, performance result or site condition.

### Engagement without clickbait
- Use a specific, useful opening idea instead of generic slogans. Headlines should create curiosity while telling the reader what they will learn.
- Keep one idea per slide. Prefer concrete verbs and short sentences over vague words such as premium, revolutionary or perfect.
- Provide a clear save-worthy takeaway: a useful question, a design check, a simple comparison or a practical trade-off.
- Use a natural Marathi + English caption, not literal word-for-word translation if that sounds awkward. End with one relevant, non-pushy prompt such as “Which would matter most in your space: shade, privacy or easy maintenance?”
- Use only a small number of relevant hashtags; do not stuff captions with broad unrelated tags.
- Never use fear-mongering, unsupported environmental claims, invented statistics, guaranteed savings, guaranteed lifespans or promises of a 100-year building.
- When discussing costs, safety, structure, fire, waterproofing, accessibility or local regulations, label the content as general education and defer site-specific decisions to qualified local professionals.

### Daily freshness and editorial review
- Keep a concept history with date, pillar, typology, headline, key insight and visual motif. Before generating the next post, avoid repeating the last 30 topics and the last 10 headline patterns when alternatives exist.
- Review recent output images and captions for visual repetition, legibility, useful contrast, natural-looking imagery, Marathi font shaping, attribution, and correct contact placement.
- Vary camera/diagram language across days: context view, threshold detail, plan-like schematic, shaded section, material close-up, landscape edge or spatial sequence. Never use the same layout as a template every day without a reason.
- Every post should look intentional as a set, but visual consistency must come from typography, spacing and palette—not from repeating the same composition.
- Label concept sketches/renders as concepts and stock/reference photography as reference imagery. Never imply that illustrative content is a completed ARCSTUDIO91 project.

## Daily Reel requirement
Create exactly one vertical Instagram Reel per daily build in addition to the four-slide carousel. Target 1080×1920, H.264 MP4, 30 fps, approximately 20–30 seconds, with a cover JPG, Marathi-first on-screen copy, SRT subtitles, and copy-ready caption. Use gentle original audio created locally or an individually verified licence; do not scrape trending music or use copyrighted tracks without permission. Keep the Reel sound-off understandable.

Avoid robotic Marathi TTS. Unless a natural, properly licensed free neural voice can be run and quality-checked in the workflow, use Marathi on-screen storytelling plus the original low-volume ambient sound bed and include a narration script separately. Do not pretend the narration script is an audio voiceover. If a licensed voice engine is introduced later, document the engine/model and its output-use licence and provide a silent-safe fallback.

The Reel story arc should be: clear hook → one useful design idea → practical insight → material/maintenance consideration → useful reader question. Make each scene readable on a phone, use restrained motion/transitions, and avoid showing five unrelated topics in the same Reel. When same-day licensed photos are used in the carousel, they may be reused inside the same-day Reel with proper credits. When no usable licensed media can be verified, use original diagram/concept artwork and label it clearly.
